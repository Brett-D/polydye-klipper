Controls

The dry run homes if needed, lifts the nozzle to 20 mm and prints two test passes without firing any ink. Wipe moves the cartridge to the sponge at the right end of the X gantry, wipes it and comes back. Both move the printer; keep the bed clear. To stop the printer at once, press Emergency stop at the top of the page; Off only turns PolyDye off once the queued moves are done.

Settings polydye.cfg

Offsets are the cartridge nozzle-row centre minus the hotend nozzle, in bed coordinates. Direction trim moves the ink of reverse passes along the pass axis. The cartridge template is the nozzle waveform: color_b_late is the stock PolyDye firmware's, and black_a and black_b are the two variants for a black cartridge (try both at the nozzle check). A black template needs pass data made for a black cartridge. The cartridge bus runs at the stock firmware's clock, 2.857 MHz, so each pulse word lasts 0.35 µs. The fire pulse sets how long each nozzle is heated: longer pulses put more energy into each drop, for when the board's boost voltage is below the cartridge's. Leave it empty for the board's 6 words (2.10 µs, stock's). Live ink is limited to 2.9 µs, 100 % of the reference heater energy at this board's 12.6 V: 8 words (2.80 µs). Saving edits only the [polydye] section of polydye.cfg (and comments out a bus_clock line, which Klipper no longer takes); Klipper applies it after a restart.

Ink position live nudges

Moves where the ink lands, from the next pass on, without a restart; it also works during a print. Compare the ink with the plastic it should sit on: if the ink is 0.1 mm too far left, press +X with 0.1 mm. Reverse passes moves only the passes printed in the reverse direction, to line them up with the forward ones. Nudges are lost at the next Klipper restart unless you press Store.

Board firmware

On the board–
Bundled with this add-on–

Updates go over the board's USB cable. Everything pending is written to the SD card first, and the Klipper service is stopped for about half a minute while the board restarts into the new firmware, then started again. If the new firmware doesn't start properly, the board goes back to the previous one on its own. Not possible during a print.

This board firmware leaves USB and comes back whenever Klipper resets it (Firmware restart, and Klipper's own restart after a configuration change). That has frozen Raspberry Pi 3s. Update it once; the safest way is with the FT232 and the board's USB unplugged from the Pi (README, “Raspberry Pi 3: freezes and damaged files”). Newer firmware resets in place.

Calibration

Fire test live ink on paper

Prints a stripe for each template and fire pulse, side by side, so you can see which settings put ink down. The default is one stripe the way the stock PolyDye firmware prints: color_b_late, 6 words and its random pattern (“like stock”, with the same knobs as the spit test); add stripes such as color_b_late:8 to compare longer pulses. On paper the stock pattern needs a hot head: a warm-up per stripe fires every nozzle (e.g. 10 s, 8 words every 508 µs) right before each stripe, with only the travel to it in between. Tape paper on the bed and fit the cartridge first. The test homes if needed, lowers the nozzle to Z and puts the settings back afterwards. Stripes run along the pass axis.

Spit test live ink, no motion

Fires the way the stock PolyDye firmware does (“like stock”, a column every 508 µs) or every nozzle 1000 times a second, for a few seconds, without moving. Hold a folded paper towel under the cartridge, against the nozzles: a working cartridge leaves a coloured patch. It is also a steady load for a USB-C power meter on the board's power input (J4): compare the current with the cartridge in and out to see whether the nozzle heaters draw anything. “Like stock” fires the way the stock PolyDye firmware does: a random quarter of positions 14-85, each column twice, with color_b_late and 6 words unless you pick others; its knobs (density, repeat, colours, positions, or an exact number of heaters per fire pulse, e.g. 1 instead of up to 12) start from those values. The column period is 1000 µs unless you set one (the stock firmware's is about 508 µs; at least the column plus 20 µs, and at most 10000 columns). A warm-up first fires every nozzle for that many seconds right before the spit, in the same run, so the head doesn't cool in between: short pulses by default, up to 2.9 µs (8 words) with a heater duty of at most 0.58 % (e.g. 8 words every 508 µs).

The spit test, the signal check and the stock timing options need newer board firmware: update it under Board firmware.

Signal check cartridge out, multimeter

Finds where each cartridge signal arrives. The page holds one line HIGH at a time; it should read its rail voltage at exactly one point and about 0 V at the others. Probe where you can reach: the CD4504 output legs on the daughter board check the board's own wiring and level shifting; the pogo pins' solder joints on the interface board check the whole chain to the cartridge pads.

  1. Keep the cartridge out for the whole check.
  2. Meter on DC volts, black lead on pin 8 (GND) of either CD4504 on the daughter board.
  3. The chips: U2, next to the power JST, carries S1–S5 and DCLK on the high rail; U1, next to the ribbon header, carries D1–D3 and CSYNC on the low rail. Pin 1 is at the dot or notch; pins 1–8 run down one side and 9–16 back up the other. The outputs are U2 legs 2, 4, 6, 10, 12 and 15, and U1 legs 4, 6, 10 and 12.
  4. Start turns the boost on in dry run (nothing fires). Then press High on a signal, find the leg that reads the expected voltage, and note it (for example U2 4). The expected leg comes from the schematic draft and isn't verified yet: note what you actually find. A line goes back to LOW after 5 minutes by itself.
  5. Finish puts every line LOW and the boost off. Then save the results.

  2. Power off. Slide the interface board out of the holder with its FFC still connected, and lay it face down, so the pogo pins' solder joints on its back face up. Power on again.
  3. Meter on DC volts, black lead on pin 8 (GND) of either CD4504 on the daughter board.
  4. Start turns the boost on in dry run (nothing fires). Then press High on a signal, find the joint that reads the expected voltage, and note it: the pogo's label, or its cluster (9 or 7 pins) and position counted from the FPC end. A line goes back to LOW after 5 minutes by itself.
  5. Finish puts every line LOW and the boost off. Then check the fire lines and ground below, with the power off, and save the results.

Fire lines and ground power off, continuity

PWRA and PWRB carry the nozzle heater pulses and are never held on. Turn the power off and set the meter to continuity. Beep from the drain of the AO3401 (marked “3401”) in each pulse limiter on the daughter board to the joints: on a SOT-23 the drain is pin 3, the single pin on its own side. PWRA comes from the F3 limiter, PWRB from the F5 limiter. Then beep CD4504 pin 8 to the joints: two of them are GND.
